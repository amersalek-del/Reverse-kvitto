How the design works

The modules stack on a shared spine on the left. The spine carries every port (mains, board I/O and all GPU outputs), so all cables leave on one side. It also hides the power cables on their way up to the cards.

  • GPU module (top): the cards stand upright behind a hinged glass door, each on a PCIe 4.0 riser from the board below. Air flows along the gap in front of each card's fans, rises past the fins and leaves through the roof fans.
  • Board drawer: the motherboard lies flat on a tray that slides out to the right with the CPU cooler and RAM. The lid stays in the case.
  • Staggered (default) sizes each module to what's inside, so parts stick out. The GPU module overhangs the drawer to the right, and that overhang carries intake fans that breathe from below. The drawer sticks out past the glass, with mesh on the exposed lid, and slides out forwards. A low PSU pod sticks out at the bottom right; the PSU lies flat and breathes through the pod's mesh top.
  • Compact packs the same parts into two flush modules. The single PSU stands on its side next to the board and takes mains from the spine through a short internal extension. The GPU module drops the floor fans and takes air in from the side wall. The risers run just under the drawer lid, over the CPU cooler.
  • Stacked adds a power module underneath for one or two PSUs, plus a mesh intake band and floor fans under the cards. It's about 170 mm taller but cools better and can split the load over two supplies.

How the numbers are worked out

  • Sustained DC load = card board power (after the power limit) + CPU + 60 W for board, RAM and drives + 3 W per fan. Each supply should stay under about 80 % so short GPU power spikes have headroom; the recommended capacity is load ÷ 0.8. Above 1600 W, ATX supplies are rare and mostly meant for 230 V.
  • With two supplies, PSU A feeds the board and CPU, and every card takes all of its 8-pin plugs from one supply. Join the two with a sync adapter and plug both into the same outlet.
  • Wall draw assumes 92 % efficiency at 230 V and 90 % at 120 V. A circuit should stay under 80 % of its breaker rating.
  • Riser length follows the cable path from slot to socket, plus 20 mm, rounded up to a stock size.
  • Airflow is rough: fans are taken at 50 % of their free-air rating (120 m³/h for 140 mm, 100 m³/h for 120 mm) to allow for mesh and fins. The temperature rise is GPU heat ÷ (1.2 kJ/m³K × flow).

Card sizes

  • XFX Mercury Magnetic Air: 345 × 135 × 68 mm, 3.5-slot. XFX quotes 327 W TGP; reviews measured about 380-393 W board power, so the model budgets 390 W (TechPowerUp, Igor'sLab, The FPS Review)
  • AMD reference: 287 × 123 × 51 mm, 355 W (TechPowerUp, Tom's Hardware)
  • Sapphire NITRO+ Vapor-X: 320 × 136 × 72 mm, 420 W primary BIOS (KitGuru)
  • ASUS TUF Gaming OC: 353 × 158 × 73 mm (ASUS). ASUS publishes no board power, so the model assumes 400 W.

Slot positions, lane splits and riser lengths are typical values. Check your motherboard manual and measure before you buy risers or cut metal.
